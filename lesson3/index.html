Introduction

The rules of time travel

Only cash flows at the same time and of the same risk can be compared or combined.

To move a cash flow forward in time, compound it:

FV_N = PV \times (1+r)^N

To move a cash flow backward in time, discount it:

PV = \dfrac{FV_N}{(1+r)^N}

What r should we use?

But there is an easier way:

PV = \dfrac{C}{(1+r)^1} + \dfrac{C}{(1+r)^2} + \cdots + \dfrac{C}{(1+r)^N}

Future values

Sometimes it is useful to obtain the future value of an annuity: the FV at the end of period N of payments of C each period for N periods, if the opportunity cost is r.

FV_N = C \times \dfrac{1}{r}\left[(1+r)^N - 1\right] \quad\text{or}\quad FV_N = PV \times (1+r)^N

The FV of an annuity is computed as of time N, the same time as the last payment. The PV is computed as of one period before the first payment.

Perpetuities

Perpetuity

A perpetuity is an annuity (a level cash flow stream) that continues forever: C, C, C, …

PV_{\text{annuity}} = C \times \dfrac{1}{r}\left[1 - \dfrac{1}{(1+r)^N}\right] \quad\xrightarrow{N\to\infty}\quad PV = \dfrac{C}{r}

Note: the PV is one period before the first cash flow. Example: a perpetuity whose first payment is in year 5 has PV_4 = \frac{C_5}{r}.

Discounted cash flow

Discounted cash flow

All the examples we have seen involve discounting cash flows. This method is called DCF (discounted cash flows), and it is the general approach to valuing any asset.

  • Step 1: Lay out the time line of cash flows C1, C2, C3, …
  • Step 2: Find the return r on a similar-risk security: measure risk, then use the risk–return relationship.
  • Step 3: Discount the cash flows at r to get value.

Remember, this is short-cut 2: the asset must be priced to offer the same reward as a similar-risk security, or there will be arbitrage opportunities.

Discounted cash flow

The same three steps, four settings

Stock

Buy, hold for two years, and sell.

Cash flows: dividends and the selling price, D1, D2 + P2

r: Re, the cost of equity (required return to shareholders)

P_0 = \frac{D_1}{1+R_e} + \frac{D_2+P_2}{(1+R_e)^2}

Bond

Buy, hold for two years, and sell or get principal repayment.

Cash flows: coupons and promised repayment

r: Rd, the cost of debt (required return to lenders, the yield to maturity)

P_0 = \frac{CPN_1}{1+R_d} + \frac{CPN_2+FV_2}{(1+R_d)^2}

Firm

Operate the firm, collect cash flows, and sell.

Cash flows: free cash flows and terminal value

r: WACC, the weighted average of the after-tax cost of debt and the cost of equity

P_0 = \frac{FCF_1}{1+W} + \frac{FCF_2+TV_2}{(1+W)^2}

Project

Invest, collect free cash flow, and liquidate or sell.

Cash flows: FCF0, FCF1, FCF2 + TV2

r: WACC

P_0 = FCF_0 + \frac{FCF_1}{1+W} + \frac{FCF_2+TV_2}{(1+W)^2}

W = WACC.
